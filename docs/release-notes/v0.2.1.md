**Script reader, a new add-on.** A teleprompter right under your webcam. Open the **+** at the end of the tabs and switch on **Script reader**: it's built in, so nothing downloads, and a **Script** tab appears. Paste a script, or drop a `.txt` file on the island, and press **Read**. The line to say stays bright under the camera with the next ones dim below, moving a line at a time at the pace you set (60 to 300 words a minute). Space pauses, the arrow keys step a line or change the pace, and on Windows **Alt+Shift+S** pauses or resumes from any app. On Windows the island stays out of screen shares and recordings while the page shows; on a Mac it shows in full-screen shares. Switched off, the island is as it was: no tab and no shortcut.

**Show over games stays.** The FPS readout now stays on the island for as long as a game is in front, like an overlay. **Alt+Shift+F** hides it until the next game, and pressing it again brings it back. Staying adds about a frame of delay to the game; if you'd rather have none, pick **Peeks** (System › Games › Readout, or right-click › In full-screen apps › **Only for a few seconds**), the way 0.1.20 did it.

**Power on the System page (Windows desktops).** A new **Power** module shows what the whole PC draws, split into the CPU, the graphics card and the rest. Corsair HXi and RMi and MSI Ai power supplies connected over USB report the real numbers, with wall power, efficiency and temperature. With other power supplies it's an estimate: the CPU's and an NVIDIA card's own readings plus about 40 W for the rest. It reads only while the System page is open.

**Lyrics on the closed island in every theme.** While a song has synced lyrics, the music pill in Classic, Oyen and Glass now sings the line, as Pill does: the album art on the left, the line in the middle, the equalizer (or Oyen) on the right. It takes the width of the widest line so far, so it settles in the first verse. Songs without synced lyrics keep the compact pill.

**Japanese readings switch themselves on.** On a Mac, and on PCs without Windows' Japanese reading data (most PCs outside Japan), the first Japanese song whose kanji go unread now downloads the 3.8 MB dictionary by itself, and the song shows romaji a moment later. Switch it off in Add-ons and it stays off.

**A countdown on the Prayer page.** With the Prayer times add-on, the Prayer tab leads with the next prayer and a live countdown to it, and lays today's five prayers on one arc, with the sun or the moon where the day stands. It ticks only while the tab is open.

**On a Mac, a third of the download.** Japanese readings are an add-on on the Mac too, in its new Add-ons tab, so the Mac app drops from 10.9 MB to 3.5 MB.

**Install:** download `Oyenza-Studio-Setup.exe` and run it. It installs just for you (no admin rights) and can start with Windows. Or unzip the portable zip and run `Poni\poni.exe`. Links to `Oyen-Poni-Setup.exe` keep working: every release carries that file too.

**On a Mac:** open `Oyenza-Studio-Mac.dmg` and drag Oyenza Studio into Applications. It isn't signed with an Apple Developer ID yet, so the first time macOS warns you, click **Done**, then open **System Settings → Privacy & Security** and click **Open Anyway**.

**Upgrading:** run the new installer. It upgrades in place and keeps your settings, data, add-ons and Oyenza sign-in.

The build isn't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC". Click **More info → Run anyway**.
