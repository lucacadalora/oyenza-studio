Music

Music, with the words.

Controls for whatever is playing: Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube in your browser, or any app that shows up in Windows' media controls or the Mac's Now Playing. Lyrics scroll in time with the song, from the free LRCLIB library, or Apple Music's own synced lyrics if you have a subscription.

YouTube videos find theirs too, even when the title carries subtitle tags or puts the song name first. After an intro or a break, the waiting dots fold away the moment the next line starts.

Drag the time bar to jump anywhere in a song: the times follow as you slide, and it jumps when you let go. Since 0.1.20 that works with Apple Music too, and so does Back: the island uses Apple Music's own slider and back button. Lyrics glide into place instead of stepping a pixel at a time.

New in 0.2.1: the closed island sings along. The line being sung shows on it in Classic, Oyen and Glass, as it does in Pill. Since 0.2.3, Japanese, Korean and Chinese lines bring their reading in Latin letters along, under the line.

Read along

Read along in Korean, Japanese and Chinese.

Like Apple Music's Show Pronunciation, each word of a lyric line gets its reading in Latin letters, set small underneath: the syllables for Korean, romaji for Japanese and pinyin with tone marks for Chinese.

New in 0.2.3: on the closed island too. While a song with readings plays, the closed island is two rows tall in every look: the line as it's written, and its reading right under it, so you can sing along without opening it. A line that needs no reading sits on its own, and songs without readings keep the one-row island. Switch off Lyrics pronunciation in Settings and the readings go, on the island and on the Music page.

Japanese kanji are read with Windows' own reading analyzer. On PCs without Windows' Japanese data, kana lines read right away, and Japanese readings, a 3.8 MB add-on that reads every kanji line, switch themselves on for the first Japanese song whose kanji go unread (new in 0.2.1). That happens only while readings are on and if you never switched the add-on yourself: switched off by hand, it stays off. On a Mac, Japanese readings are an add-on in the Add-ons tab too.

Since 0.1.15 it reads closer to how the words are sung: numbers with their counters (二人 futari, 一歩 ippo), は as "wa" after every word (心には kokoro ni wa), and words like 愛おしい and 失くす. Turn it off in Settings or the tray menu.

System

Your PC at a glance.

CPU, GPU, memory, drives, network and battery in rings and charts: each core's load with performance and efficiency cores apart, the graphics card's engines and memory, memory pressure and what fills memory, read, write, download and upload speeds, and the processes using the most.

An add-on since 0.2.9: System is built in and off until you switch it on under Add-ons. Off, there's no System tab and nothing reads the counters.

It reads Windows' own kernel counters and keeps the last minute while the page is closed, so the charts are full the moment it opens. That minute rests while your PC is locked, the screen is off or battery saver is on. Keeping it takes about a third of a millisecond each second, and the tray menu turns it off. No driver, no admin rights, and nothing leaves your PC.

New in 0.2.1, on a desktop: Power. What the whole PC draws, split into the CPU, the graphics card and the rest. It comes from the power supply itself when it reports over USB (Corsair HXi and RMi, and MSI MEG and MPG Ai models), and is estimated otherwise: the CPU's and the graphics card's own readings plus about 40 W for the board, memory, drives and fans, labelled as an estimate. The graphics card's watts come from NVIDIA's driver; AMD and Intel cards aren't read yet, and without a card reading there's no estimate. It reads only while the System page is open. Laptops keep their Battery module.

On a Mac it reads macOS's own counters the way Stats does, and rests while the screen is asleep or the Mac is locked. On a Mac without a battery (a Mac mini, Studio, iMac or Mac Pro with Apple silicon), Power shows what the whole Mac draws, from its own sensor, the number Stats shows as System total; macOS doesn't split it by part. A MacBook keeps its Battery module.

Phone drop and paired phones

From your phone, from anywhere.

Phone drop: open the Shelf, click Phone and scan the QR code with your phone's camera. Pick photos or files, tap Send, and the island asks before anything arrives. It works with iPhone and Android over your own Wi-Fi: nothing to install on the phone, and nothing goes through the internet. The phone can save what's on your Shelf too.

Paired phones: click Pair a phone under Phone drop and scan the code once. After that, your phone finds this PC from anywhere: the same Wi-Fi, another Wi-Fi or mobile data, whenever Oyenza Studio is running. Files from your own phones go straight onto the Shelf, and the island tells you when they land.

Keep the page on your iPhone's Home Screen, or install it on Android, where Oyenza Studio joins the share sheet. The phone and the PC meet through drop.oyenza.com, and the files go straight between them over an encrypted link. Paired phones lists each phone with Remove.

Prayer times · An add-on

Prayer times, for your city.

Today's times as Kemenag RI publishes them for your kab/kota, the next prayer and how long until it, and the qibla. New in 0.2.1: the page counts down to the next prayer in hours, minutes and seconds, and shows today's five prayers on one arc, with the sun or the moon. New in 0.2.10: anywhere in the world. Type any city, or let the location find it: 33,000 cities, each with the method its country follows, on its own clock, summer time included. Your computer's location finds the nearest of Kemenag's 517 places, from Windows' location or the Mac's Location Services, and checks again when your laptop travels, or you type your city. The point stays on your computer: it only picks the place.

In the ten minutes before each prayer the island counts down, and when it's time it says so, with a short tone, the adzan recorded at Masjidil Haram, or nothing. Checked against Kemenag's 2026 schedule: 98.2% of 227,367 times to the minute, none more than a minute off. Makkah, Madinah and Jeddah use Umm al-Qura.

New in 0.2.3: on the Mac. Prayer times is in the Mac's Add-ons tab too, with the adzan. Around a MacBook's notch the countdown sits beside the camera: the moon on the left, the minutes on the right.

Prayer times is an add-on: open the + at the end of the tabs and switch it on. It downloads then, 1.9 MB, checked against the fingerprint built into the app, and switching it off deletes its files.

New in 0.2.1 · An add-on

A teleprompter, under your webcam.

Paste a script, or drop a .txt or .md file on the island while the Script page shows. The line to say stays bright, with the next ones dim below it, the way the Music page shows lyrics, and the reader moves a line at a time at the pace you pick: 60 to 300 words a minute, 140 to start.

Click the script to steer it from the keyboard: Space pauses, ← and → step back or on a line, ↑ and ↓ change the pace, and Esc lets go. On Windows, Alt+Shift+S pauses or resumes it from any app.

On Windows, the island is left out of screen shares and screen captures while the Script page shows, so the people watching don't see your lines. On a Mac it shows in full-screen shares: macOS no longer lets an app keep itself out of them.

Script reader is an add-on that's built into the app, so there's nothing to download: open the + at the end of the tabs and switch it on, and a Script tab appears. Switched off, the island is as it was: no Script tab, no shortcut and nothing running for it. It's in the Mac's Add-ons tab too.

Show over games

FPS, GPU and CPU, over your games.

While a game fills the screen in borderless full-screen, the island becomes a small readout: frames per second, the graphics card's load and temperature, and the CPU's load. New in 0.2.1, it stays over the game for as long as the game is in front, like the overlays graphics apps have. Alt+Shift+F hides it until the next game, and pressing it again brings it back.

Staying adds about a frame of delay: any window over a borderless game makes Windows combine the two before they reach the screen. If you'd rather have no added delay, pick Peeks: System › Games › Readout › Peeks, or right-click the island › In full-screen apps › Only for a few seconds. The readout then shows for a few seconds when the game comes to the front, when the frame rate drops sharply, and when you press Alt+Shift+F.

Switch it on in System › Games, or right-click the island › In full-screen apps › Show FPS, GPU and CPU over games.

Frames are counted the way Intel's PresentMon and Xbox Game Bar count them, from Windows' own log of each frame a game shows. Nothing is loaded into the game, so anti-cheat has nothing to see. DirectX 9 to 12, Vulkan and OpenGL. The first time, Windows asks once for an admin OK, as Game Bar's FPS counter does, and FPS shows from your next sign-in. Over a full-screen app, Glass draws smoked glass instead of copying the screen.

Stays on top: when another window covers the island, such as a browser left on top after full screen or an app set to stay on top, the island goes back on top. In full-screen games and videos you choose: right-click the island or open Settings, then In full-screen apps › Hide or Stay on top (on a Mac, the menu bar under Oyen's mark). Hide is the default. A game in exclusive full-screen mode still covers every window.

Over maximized windows it stays. The island sits over the middle of the top edge, so over a maximized browser it can cover the tabs there. To keep that spot clear, pick Hidden until hover as the idle look: while nothing plays, only an invisible strip stays at the top edge until you point at it. (0.2.4 and 0.2.5 stepped aside over maximized windows by default, which made the island seem to vanish; 0.2.6 drops that.)

Oyen

Oyen takes over the island.

The island stays black and quiet by default. Switch on the Oyen theme and the whole island turns Oyen: warm cream, dark brown text, his orange on the controls, and Oyen himself peeking over the edge, the same Oyen as in the Oyenza app.

  • Cream and brown on every page, with Oyen's orange on the play button, the progress and volume bars and the switches.
  • Every Latin letter on the island is set in Outfit, which ships inside the app.
  • Built from the Oyenza logo, so he stays sharp at every size and scaling. On an open page he peeks up beside the pin, eyes on your cursor. Resting, he blinks now and then and watches your pointer when it comes near.
  • Music notes float up beside Oyen on every beat, and he sways along.
  • He moves only when something happens and sleeps after midnight, so he costs no CPU while nothing moves.

Open the island and press the gear, then Theme › Oyen. Switch back to Classic any time.

Glass

The island, as liquid glass.

Its edge bends and magnifies what's under it, the middle is frosted, a light runs along the rim, and it turns light or dark with what's behind it. The open island follows Windows' light or dark mode.

  • To draw the glass, Oyenza Studio copies the part of the screen behind its window (the island and the space it opens into) while Glass is on, and not while the island is hidden or the PC is locked. The copy stays in your PC's memory, each new copy replaces the last, and it's never saved or sent.
  • While Glass is on, Windows leaves the island out of screenshots, recordings and screen sharing.
  • Glass also works on PCs whose colour settings make Windows draw the desktop in 16-bit colour, and on HDR displays. Where it can't copy the screen (Remote Desktop, Windows 10 before version 2004, some laptops), Glass draws smoked glass instead: the same shape and light over a see-through tint, and it stays in screenshots.
  • Classic stays the default look.

Open the island and press the gear, then Theme › Glass.

New in 0.1.21

Pill. The island floats.

Pill changes the island's shape, not just its colour. It floats a little below the top of the screen as a black capsule, with layouts of its own: the time and battery while it rests, the song while music plays, and a rounded card when it opens.

  • Resting: the time on the left and, on a laptop, the battery on the right, with a bolt while it's plugged in. A desktop shows the time alone.
  • Status bar is Pill's own idle look: the volume, the date and time in an inner pill, and the battery. Right-click › Idle look › Status bar. The other themes keep the clock.
  • Music playing: the line being sung, or the title and artist between lines, with bars in the album's colour and a glow of it along the bottom.
  • Focus timer, pop-ups and notices take the same capsule shape; notices get a taller capsule with room for two lines.
  • Open: a card with every corner rounded and Classic's tabs.
  • A soft shadow and a fine rim keep it apart from the wallpaper, light or dark. Colour only where it means something: the album's colour for music, green for a charging battery, red for a low one.
  • The pages and features are the same as in Classic. Classic stays the default look.

Right-click the island, then Theme › Pill. Or open the island, press the gear and pick Pill under Theme. On a Mac, it's in the menu bar's Theme menu.

On a Mac without a notch, or on another display, it hangs from the top of the screen as it does on Windows. Around the notch, the Oyen theme goes black like the notch itself, with Oyen's cream type, his orange and his face on it.

  • Whatever plays: the Music and Spotify apps, YouTube, YouTube Music or Spotify in your browser, and any other player in the Mac's Now Playing, with synced lyrics from LRCLIB, or Apple Music's own with your token, and the reading under Japanese, Korean and Chinese lines, on the closed island too.
  • The focus timer, the Shelf with Phone drop over the same Wi-Fi, tasks, and the System add-on with your Mac's CPU, graphics, memory, drives, network, battery and busiest apps.
  • Pop-ups for volume, charging and Bluetooth headphones (without the battery level; macOS shows AirPods' own when they connect), the Classic, Oyen, Glass and Pill themes, and start at login. Every option is in the menu bar, under Oyen's mark.
  • Full-screen apps: while a video, a game or a slideshow is full screen, the island stays out of its way and comes back after, as on Windows. Or it stays on top: the menu bar's In full-screen apps, or Settings.
  • Oyenza sign-in: money in and out, bills due and your last 7 days on the island, as on Windows. The Mac's key stays in a file only your Mac account can read.
  • Glass is macOS's own glass, Liquid Glass on macOS 26 and a dark blur before it, with a black strip where it meets the notch.
  • Pill: on a MacBook with a notch, the resting island goes back into the notch; music, the timer, the prayer countdown, pop-ups, notices and the open island float as the pill just below it. Without a notch it floats below the top of the screen, as on Windows.

Oyenza account · On Windows and Mac · Steps new in 0.2.5

Your Oyenza, at the top of your screen.

Sign in with your oyenza.app account the way you sign in on a TV: press the gear, choose Sign in with Oyenza, and approve the code the island shows at oyenza.app/connect. No password is ever typed into Oyenza Studio. An Oyenza page then joins the island.

  • Your Oyenza, live, by day. Every transaction of the last 7 days, money in and out, newest first. Pick a day on the strip, and what's logged while you look glows.
  • Money in and out as it happens. A notice when Oyenza logs money coming in or going out, with the amount hidden until you click Show.
  • Bills before they're due. Subscriptions and card due dates for the next 30 days, and a reminder the day before each one.
  • Note a spend in a second. Type "kopi 25rb" on the Oyenza page and press Enter: it goes straight to Oyenza, with 10 seconds to undo.
  • Your steps. In Oyenza on your phone, turn on Show on Oyenza Studio under your steps in Health (Sehat), and the Health page shows your daily steps, distance and active energy for the last 7 days, with a notice when you reach your goal.

Signing in is optional. This computer gets a key of its own, never your password: it sees your last 7 days of transactions and the bills due, and saves the spends you type: never balances, account numbers, notes or receipts. With Show on Oyenza Studio on, it also gets your daily step totals for the last 7 days, kept in memory only; sleep, heart rate and weight stay on your phone. Disconnect it in the island's Settings, or in oyenza.app under Connected apps.

Windows 10 and 11

Ready in under a minute.

  1. Download the installer: Oyenza-Studio-Setup.exe, from the latest release on GitHub. A portable zip is on the Releases page too.
  2. Run it. It installs just for you and needs no admin rights.
  3. If Windows warns you: builds aren't code-signed yet, so SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC". Choose More info, then Run anyway. If Smart App Control is on, Windows won't run unsigned apps at all; a Microsoft Store version is on the way.

Where things live

Your data: %APPDATA%\Poni\. Cache and log: %LOCALAPPDATA%\Poni\. Uninstalling removes the app, cache and log, and keeps your data.

  1. Download the app: Oyenza-Studio-Mac.dmg, from the latest release. One app for Apple silicon and Intel.
  2. Drag it into Applications. Open the DMG: its window shows Oyenza Studio and your Applications folder. Drag the one onto the other.
  3. Allow it the first time: it isn't signed with an Apple Developer ID yet, so macOS warns you the first time you open it. Click Done, then open System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.

Where things live

Your data: ~/Library/Application Support/Poni/. Log: ~/Library/Logs/Poni/.
